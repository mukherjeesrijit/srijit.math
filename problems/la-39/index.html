Linear Algebra · Hard · Linear Maps · Problem 2

Splitting a surjection

Let n,m and p be positive integers and suppose g:Rm→Rp is a linear map which is onto.

(a) Prove that there exists a linear map h:Rp→Rm such that h is one to one and g(h(x))=x for all x∈Rp.

(b) Suppose f:Rn→Rm is a linear map which is one to one and Ran(f)=Ker(g). Prove that Ran(f)∩Ran(h)={0}.

(c) Prove that Rm={f(v)+h(x):v∈Rn,x∈Rp}.

(Here Ran(f)=f(X) is the range of f.)

Source: ISI MMath
