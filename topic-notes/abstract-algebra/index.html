Abstract Algebra

1. Groups and subgroups

A group is a set with an associative operation, an identity e, and inverses. (ab)−1=b−1a−1.

  • Subgroup test: H=∅ and a,b∈H⇒ab−1∈H. For finite H, closure is enough.
  • Order of an element: the least n≥1 with gn=e. gk=e⟺o(g)∣k; o(gk)=gcd(k,o(g))o(g)​; o(ab)=o(ba) (conjugates).
  • Abelian tests: G is abelian iff g↦g−1 is a homomorphism iff g↦g2 is a homomorphism; if g2=e for all g, G is abelian.
  • Cancelling relations: from xayb=e and xcyd=e, eliminate step by step: e.g. x5y3=x8y5=e forces x=y=e.
  • Examples: Zn​, Zn×​ (order φ(n)), Sn​, An​, dihedral Dn​ (order 2n), matrix groups GLn​, SLn​, On​, quaternions Q8​.
  • Subgroups of Z2k​: subspaces of F2k​; Z23​ has 1+7+7+1=16.
  • Closed subgroups of (R,+): {0}, aZ, or R; other subgroups (e.g. Z+2​Z) are dense.
  • Product of subgroups: ∣HK∣=∣H∩K∣∣H∣∣K∣​.
  • Class equation: ∣G∣=∣Z(G)∣+∑[G:C(gi​)]. Only Z2​ has exactly two conjugacy classes.

2. Cyclic and permutation groups

  • Cyclic groups: Zn​ has exactly one subgroup of each order d∣n, cyclic, and φ(d) elements of order d. Subgroups and quotients of cyclic groups are cyclic.
  • Zm​×Zn​≅Zmn​ iff gcd(m,n)=1. The order of (a1​,…,ak​) is the lcm of the orders.
  • Hom from cyclic groups: a homomorphism Zn​→G is fixed by the image of 1, which can be any g with gn=e.
  • Permutations: write as disjoint cycles; the order is the lcm of the cycle lengths; the sign is (−1)n−#cycles.
  • Conjugacy in Sn​ = same cycle type. xk is conjugate to x for all x∈Sn​ iff gcd(k,ℓ)=1 for every cycle length ℓ≤n.
  • Counting: the number of k-cycles in Sn​ is (kn​)(k−1)!; elements of order 6 in S5​ are (abc)(de): 20, giving 10 cyclic subgroups.
  • Generators: Sn​=⟨(12),(12⋯n)⟩; An​ is generated by 3-cycles.
  • Cayley: every group of order n embeds in Sn​.
  • Dihedral groups: two reflections in lines at angle kπ​ generate Dk​, of order 2k; two reflections can generate an infinite group.

3. Lagrange and Sylow

  • Lagrange: ∣H∣ divides ∣G∣; so o(g)∣∣G∣ and g∣G∣=e. Groups of prime order are cyclic. (The converse fails: A4​ has no subgroup of order 6.)
  • Cauchy: if a prime p∣∣G∣, G has an element of order p.
  • Groups of order p2 are abelian: Zp2​ or Zp2​. p-groups have nontrivial centre.
  • Sylow theorems (∣G∣=pam, p∤m): Sylow p-subgroups exist, are all conjugate, and their number np​ satisfies np​≡1(modp) and np​∣m. np​=1 iff the Sylow subgroup is normal.
  • Using Sylow: list the divisors of m that are ≡1(modp). Order 15, 35, pq with p∤q−1: cyclic. Order 30: n5​∈{1,6}, n3​∈{1,10}, and in fact both are 1.
  • Counting elements: distinct subgroups of prime order p meet trivially, so np​ of them give np​(p−1) elements of order p.
  • Index 2 subgroups are normal. A subgroup of index p (smallest prime dividing ∣G∣) is normal.
  • Finite abelian groups: a direct product of cyclic p-power groups; the number of abelian groups of order p1a1​​⋯ is ∏P(ai​) (partition numbers). A subgroup need not split off: Zp2​≅Zp​×Zp​.

4. Homomorphisms and quotients

  • Homomorphism: φ(ab)=φ(a)φ(b). Images of subgroups are subgroups; images of normal subgroups are normal in the image, not necessarily in the target. o(φ(g))∣o(g).
  • Normal subgroups: gNg−1=N. Kernels are normal. Any subgroup containing [G,G] is normal, and G/N is abelian iff N⊇[G,G].
  • First isomorphism theorem: G/kerφ≅Imφ. E.g. upper triangular (a0​ba−1​) modulo b-part ≅(R+,⋅).
  • Second: HK/K≅H/(H∩K) (with K normal). Third: (G/K)/(H/K)≅G/H.
  • Correspondence theorem: subgroups of G/N ↔ subgroups of G containing N.
  • Normaliser and centraliser: NG​(H)={g:gHg−1=H}; the diagonal torus in invertible upper triangular matrices is its own normaliser.
  • Images in abelian groups: a homomorphism from Sn​ into an abelian group kills An​ (the commutator subgroup).

5. Rings and ideals

All rings commutative with 1 unless stated.

  • Ideal: an additive subgroup with rI⊆I. I+J, IJ, I∩J are ideals; IJ⊆I∩J.
  • Comaximal: I+J=R. Then Im+Jn=R, and I∩J=IJ.
  • Chinese remainder theorem: if I1​,…,Ik​ are pairwise comaximal, R/(I1​⋯Ik​)≅∏R/Ij​. E.g. R[x]/(x2−1)≅R×R.
  • Prime ideal: ab∈P⇒a∈P or b∈P; R/P is an integral domain. Maximal: R/M is a field. Maximal ⇒ prime.
  • Radical and nilradical: I​={a:an∈I} is an ideal. The nilradical is the intersection of all prime ideals.
  • Units: in Z[−d​] (d>1) the norm a2+db2=1 gives only ±1. In k[x]/(x2) the units are a+bx with a=0.
  • Boolean rings (a2=a): 2a=0 and commutative; e.g. Z2n​.
  • Finite commutative rings are products of local rings; an integral domain that is finite is a field. Z4​ is local, not a field, with cyclic units.
  • Small rings: rings of order p or p2 are commutative; upper triangular 2×2 matrices over Fp​ give a noncommutative ring of order p3.
  • Ring homomorphisms out of R[x]/I are fixed by the image of x, which must satisfy the relations in I.

6. Polynomial rings and fields

  • k[x] (k a field) is a PID: every ideal is (f); k[x]/(f) is a field iff f is irreducible.
  • Z[x] is not a PID: (2,x) needs two generators. To find the minimal number of generators, simplify the generators (e.g. (11x11,13x7,17x5,19x3)=(19x3,x5)) and check whether one element can divide all of them.
  • Irreducibility tests: Eisenstein; reduction mod p; rational roots; degree 2,3: irreducible iff no root. Over R irreducibles have degree 1 or 2, so R[x]/(quartic) is never a domain.
  • Field extensions: [K:F]=dimF​K; [L:F]=[L:K][K:F]. Q(α)≅Q[x]/(mα​); conjugate roots give isomorphic fields, e.g. three copies of Q(32​) in C.
  • Finite fields: Fq​ exists iff q=pn; Fq×​ is cyclic; aq=a. The number of lines in Fqn​ is q−1qn−1​.
  • Field homomorphisms are injective but need not be onto (Q(t)→Q(t), t↦t2).
  • Linear systems over Zp​: unique solution iff the determinant is nonzero mod p.
  • Fermat/Euler: ap−1≡1(modp); aφ(n)≡1(modn) for gcd(a,n)=1.
