Abstract Algebra

1. Groups and subgroups

A group is a set with an associative operation, an identity $e$, and inverses. $(ab)^{-1}=b^{-1}a^{-1}$.

  • Subgroup test: $H\ne\emptyset$ and $a,b\in H\Rightarrow ab^{-1}\in H$. For finite $H$, closure is enough.
  • Order of an element: the least $n\ge1$ with $g^n=e$. $g^k=e\iff o(g)\mid k$; $o(g^k)=\frac{o(g)}{\gcd(k,o(g))}$; $o(ab)=o(ba)$ (conjugates).
  • Abelian tests: $G$ is abelian iff $g\mapsto g^{-1}$ is a homomorphism iff $g\mapsto g^2$ is a homomorphism; if $g^2=e$ for all $g$, $G$ is abelian.
  • Cancelling relations: from $x^ay^b=e$ and $x^cy^d=e$, eliminate step by step: e.g. $x^5y^3=x^8y^5=e$ forces $x=y=e$.
  • Examples: $\mathbb Z_n$, $\mathbb Z_n^\times$ (order $\varphi(n)$), $S_n$, $A_n$, dihedral $D_n$ (order $2n$), matrix groups $GL_n$, $SL_n$, $O_n$, quaternions $Q_8$.
  • Subgroups of $\mathbb Z_2^k$: subspaces of $\mathbb F_2^k$; $\mathbb Z_2^3$ has $1+7+7+1=16$.
  • Closed subgroups of $(\mathbb R,+)$: $\{0\}$, $a\mathbb Z$, or $\mathbb R$; other subgroups (e.g. $\mathbb Z+\sqrt2\mathbb Z$) are dense.
  • Product of subgroups: $|HK|=\frac{|H||K|}{|H\cap K|}$.
  • Class equation: $|G|=|Z(G)|+\sum[G:C(g_i)]$. Only $\mathbb Z_2$ has exactly two conjugacy classes.

2. Cyclic and permutation groups

  • Cyclic groups: $\mathbb Z_n$ has exactly one subgroup of each order $d\mid n$, cyclic, and $\varphi(d)$ elements of order $d$. Subgroups and quotients of cyclic groups are cyclic.
  • $\mathbb Z_m\times\mathbb Z_n\cong\mathbb Z_{mn}$ iff $\gcd(m,n)=1$. The order of $(a_1,\dots,a_k)$ is the lcm of the orders.
  • Hom from cyclic groups: a homomorphism $\mathbb Z_n\to G$ is fixed by the image of $1$, which can be any $g$ with $g^n=e$.
  • Permutations: write as disjoint cycles; the order is the lcm of the cycle lengths; the sign is $(-1)^{n-\#\text{cycles}}$.
  • Conjugacy in $S_n$ $=$ same cycle type. $x^k$ is conjugate to $x$ for all $x\in S_n$ iff $\gcd(k,\ell)=1$ for every cycle length $\ell\le n$.
  • Counting: the number of $k$-cycles in $S_n$ is $\binom nk(k-1)!$; elements of order $6$ in $S_5$ are $(abc)(de)$: $20$, giving $10$ cyclic subgroups.
  • Generators: $S_n=\langle(1\,2),(1\,2\cdots n)\rangle$; $A_n$ is generated by $3$-cycles.
  • Cayley: every group of order $n$ embeds in $S_n$.
  • Dihedral groups: two reflections in lines at angle $\frac\pi k$ generate $D_k$, of order $2k$; two reflections can generate an infinite group.

3. Lagrange and Sylow

  • Lagrange: $|H|$ divides $|G|$; so $o(g)\mid|G|$ and $g^{|G|}=e$. Groups of prime order are cyclic. (The converse fails: $A_4$ has no subgroup of order $6$.)
  • Cauchy: if a prime $p\mid|G|$, $G$ has an element of order $p$.
  • Groups of order $p^2$ are abelian: $\mathbb Z_{p^2}$ or $\mathbb Z_p^2$. $p$-groups have nontrivial centre.
  • Sylow theorems ($|G|=p^am$, $p\nmid m$): Sylow $p$-subgroups exist, are all conjugate, and their number $n_p$ satisfies $n_p\equiv1\pmod p$ and $n_p\mid m$. $n_p=1$ iff the Sylow subgroup is normal.
  • Using Sylow: list the divisors of $m$ that are $\equiv1\pmod p$. Order $15$, $35$, $pq$ with $p\nmid q-1$: cyclic. Order $30$: $n_5\in\{1,6\}$, $n_3\in\{1,10\}$, and in fact both are $1$.
  • Counting elements: distinct subgroups of prime order $p$ meet trivially, so $n_p$ of them give $n_p(p-1)$ elements of order $p$.
  • Index 2 subgroups are normal. A subgroup of index $p$ (smallest prime dividing $|G|$) is normal.
  • Finite abelian groups: a direct product of cyclic $p$-power groups; the number of abelian groups of order $p_1^{a_1}\cdots$ is $\prod P(a_i)$ (partition numbers). A subgroup need not split off: $\mathbb Z_{p^2}\not\cong\mathbb Z_p\times\mathbb Z_p$.

4. Homomorphisms and quotients

  • Homomorphism: $\varphi(ab)=\varphi(a)\varphi(b)$. Images of subgroups are subgroups; images of normal subgroups are normal in the image, not necessarily in the target. $o(\varphi(g))\mid o(g)$.
  • Normal subgroups: $gNg^{-1}=N$. Kernels are normal. Any subgroup containing $[G,G]$ is normal, and $G/N$ is abelian iff $N\supseteq[G,G]$.
  • First isomorphism theorem: $G/\ker\varphi\cong\operatorname{Im}\varphi$. E.g. upper triangular $\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&a^{-1}\end{pmatrix}$ modulo $b$-part $\cong(\mathbb R^+,\cdot)$.
  • Second: $HK/K\cong H/(H\cap K)$ (with $K$ normal). Third: $(G/K)/(H/K)\cong G/H$.
  • Correspondence theorem: subgroups of $G/N$ $\leftrightarrow$ subgroups of $G$ containing $N$.
  • Normaliser and centraliser: $N_G(H)=\{g:gHg^{-1}=H\}$; the diagonal torus in invertible upper triangular matrices is its own normaliser.
  • Images in abelian groups: a homomorphism from $S_n$ into an abelian group kills $A_n$ (the commutator subgroup).

5. Rings and ideals

All rings commutative with $1$ unless stated.

  • Ideal: an additive subgroup with $rI\subseteq I$. $I+J$, $IJ$, $I\cap J$ are ideals; $IJ\subseteq I\cap J$.
  • Comaximal: $I+J=R$. Then $I^m+J^n=R$, and $I\cap J=IJ$.
  • Chinese remainder theorem: if $I_1,\dots,I_k$ are pairwise comaximal, $R/(I_1\cdots I_k)\cong\prod R/I_j$. E.g. $\mathbb R[x]/(x^2-1)\cong\mathbb R\times\mathbb R$.
  • Prime ideal: $ab\in P\Rightarrow a\in P$ or $b\in P$; $R/P$ is an integral domain. Maximal: $R/M$ is a field. Maximal $\Rightarrow$ prime.
  • Radical and nilradical: $\sqrt I=\{a:a^n\in I\}$ is an ideal. The nilradical is the intersection of all prime ideals.
  • Units: in $\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-d}]$ ($d>1$) the norm $a^2+db^2=1$ gives only $\pm1$. In $k[x]/(x^2)$ the units are $a+bx$ with $a\ne0$.
  • Boolean rings ($a^2=a$): $2a=0$ and commutative; e.g. $\mathbb Z_2^n$.
  • Finite commutative rings are products of local rings; an integral domain that is finite is a field. $\mathbb Z_4$ is local, not a field, with cyclic units.
  • Small rings: rings of order $p$ or $p^2$ are commutative; upper triangular $2\times2$ matrices over $\mathbb F_p$ give a noncommutative ring of order $p^3$.
  • Ring homomorphisms out of $R[x]/I$ are fixed by the image of $x$, which must satisfy the relations in $I$.

6. Polynomial rings and fields

  • $k[x]$ ($k$ a field) is a PID: every ideal is $(f)$; $k[x]/(f)$ is a field iff $f$ is irreducible.
  • $\mathbb Z[x]$ is not a PID: $(2,x)$ needs two generators. To find the minimal number of generators, simplify the generators (e.g. $(11x^{11},13x^7,17x^5,19x^3)=(19x^3,x^5)$) and check whether one element can divide all of them.
  • Irreducibility tests: Eisenstein; reduction mod $p$; rational roots; degree $2,3$: irreducible iff no root. Over $\mathbb R$ irreducibles have degree $1$ or $2$, so $\mathbb R[x]/(\text{quartic})$ is never a domain.
  • Field extensions: $[K:F]=\dim_FK$; $[L:F]=[L:K][K:F]$. $\mathbb Q(\alpha)\cong\mathbb Q[x]/(m_\alpha)$; conjugate roots give isomorphic fields, e.g. three copies of $\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2)$ in $\mathbb C$.
  • Finite fields: $\mathbb F_q$ exists iff $q=p^n$; $\mathbb F_q^\times$ is cyclic; $a^q=a$. The number of lines in $\mathbb F_q^n$ is $\frac{q^n-1}{q-1}$.
  • Field homomorphisms are injective but need not be onto ($\mathbb Q(t)\to\mathbb Q(t)$, $t\mapsto t^2$).
  • Linear systems over $\mathbb Z_p$: unique solution iff the determinant is nonzero mod $p$.
  • Fermat/Euler: $a^{p-1}\equiv1\pmod p$; $a^{\varphi(n)}\equiv1\pmod n$ for $\gcd(a,n)=1$.
