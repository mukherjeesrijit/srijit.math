Algebra

1. Foundations & linear systems

  • Absolute value: ∣x∣=max(x,−x); ∣x−a∣ is the distance from x to a. ∣x∣<r⟺−r<x<r. Solve ∣f∣=∣g∣ by f=±g, or by cases at the points where the insides change sign.
  • Sums of distances: ∑∣x−ai​∣ is least at a median of the ai​ (anywhere between the two middle ones for an even count).
  • Linear inequalities in two variables describe half-planes; a linear expression is extreme at a corner of the region.
  • Linear systems: ax+by=e, cx+dy=f has a unique solution iff ad−bc=0 (Cramer: x=ad−bced−bf​). Otherwise there are none or infinitely many.
  • Functional equations with f(−x): put −x for x to get a second equation and solve the linear system (e.g. 2f(x)+3f(−x)=15−4x).
  • Sets: A∖B=A∩Bc; De Morgan (A∪B)c=Ac∩Bc; ∣A∪B∣=∣A∣+∣B∣−∣A∩B∣.

2. Polynomials

  • Remainder theorem: p(x)=(x−a)q(x)+p(a). Factor theorem: (x−a)∣p(x)⟺p(a)=0.
  • Roots of unity as test points: x2+x+1 divides p iff p(ω)=0 for a primitive cube root ω (use ω3=1, 1+ω+ω2=0).
  • Vieta: for x3+ax2+bx+c with roots ri​: ∑ri​=−a, ∑ri​rj​=b, r1​r2​r3​=−c. Power sums follow by Newton: pk​=e1​pk−1​−e2​pk−2​+e3​pk−3​.
  • Counting real roots: sign changes (Descartes), values at chosen points (IVT), and turning points (p′): between two roots there is a root of p′ (Rolle).
  • Rational roots: a root qp​ in lowest terms of an integer polynomial has p∣a0​, q∣an​.
  • Integer polynomials: a−b∣p(a)−p(b).
  • Identities: a polynomial of degree ≤n vanishing at n+1 points is 0; Lagrange interpolation ∑i​yi​∏j=i​xi​−xj​x−xj​​ is the polynomial through given points.
  • Symmetric polynomial equations (x4+ax3+bx2+ax+1): divide by x2 and put t=x+x1​.

3. Quadratics

  • ax2+bx+c=a(x+2ab​)2−4ab2−4ac​: vertex at x=−2ab​.
  • Discriminant D=b2−4ac: two real roots, a double root, or none as D>0, =0, <0.
  • Roots α,β: α+β=−ab​, αβ=ac​. "One root twice the other": β=2α gives 2b2=9ac.
  • Sign of a quadratic: with a>0 it is negative exactly between the roots; ax2+bx+c>0 for all x iff a>0 and D<0.
  • Location of roots: both roots exceed k iff D≥0, af(k)>0 and −2ab​>k.
  • Equations in ∣x∣ or x2: substitute t=∣x∣ or t=x2, solve for t≥0, then count the x.
  • A quadratic squeezed between bounds: 0≤f≤(x−1)2 forces f(1)=0 to be a minimum, so f=a(x−1)2.

4. Exponents, radicals & logarithms

  • aman=am+n, (am)n=amn, am/n=nam​.
  • Conjugates: (a+b​)n+(a−b​)n is an integer; if 0<a−b​<1, it gives the integer part of (a+b​)n.
  • Telescoping geometric sums: 1+t+⋯+tn−1=t−1tn−1​ (e.g. with t=21/5).
  • Radical equations: substitute (u=4x​), square carefully and check for extra roots.
  • Logarithms: log(ab)=loga+logb, loga​b=logalogb​, alogb​c=clogb​a.

5. Rational expressions

  • Inequalities QP​>0: mark the zeros of P and Q on the line and read off the signs; never multiply by Q unless its sign is known.
  • Partial fractions: k(k+1)1​=k1​−k+11​; repeated and quadratic factors take (x−a)2A​, x2+px+qBx+C​ terms.
  • Range of dx2+ex+fax2+bx+c​: set it equal to y and require a real x (discriminant ≥0), or substitute u=(x−h)2.
  • Symmetric conditions: a1​+b1​+c1​=a+b+c1​ is the same as (a+b)(b+c)(c+a)=0.

6. Functions, sequences & series

  • AP: an​=a+(n−1)d, Sn​=2n​(a1​+an​). GP: an​=arn−1, Sn​=ar−1rn−1​, S∞​=1−ra​ for ∣r∣<1.
  • AGP: ∑n≥1​nxn=(1−x)2x​ for ∣x∣<1 (differentiate the geometric series, or multiply by x and subtract).
  • Telescoping: write the term as uk​−uk+1​; the sum is u1​−un+1​.
  • Means: for positive a,b: AM=2a+b​≥GM=ab​≥HM=a+b2ab​, and GM2=AM⋅HM.
  • Sequences from totals: if Sn​ is given, an​=Sn​−Sn−1​.
  • Iterated maps: f(x)=ax+b has f(n)(x)=anx+ba−1an−1​.
  • Recursive definitions: compute small cases, guess the pattern, prove by induction.

7. Inequalities

  • AM–GM: nx1​+⋯+xn​​≥nx1​⋯xn​​, equality iff all equal. To maximise xy2z3 with x+y+z fixed, split as x+2y​+2y​+3z​+3z​+3z​.
  • Cauchy–Schwarz: (∑ai​bi​)2≤∑ai2​∑bi2​, equality iff proportional. Consequence: ∑xi​​≤n∑xi​​ and ∑xi2​≥n(∑xi​)2​.
  • Cyclic sums: a2​a1​​+a3​a2​​+⋯+a1​an​​≥n (AM–GM, the product is 1).
  • Triangle inequality: ∣a+b∣≤∣a∣+∣b∣; for complex numbers too.
  • Rearrangement: ∑ai​bi​ is largest when both are sorted the same way.
  • Squares are non-negative: many inequalities are ∑(something)2≥0, e.g. a2+b2+c2≥ab+bc+ca.
  • Recurrences: for un+1​=un​+un​1​, square: un+12​=un2​+2+un2​1​, then bound the sum.
