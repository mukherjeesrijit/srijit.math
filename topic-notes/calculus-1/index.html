1. Limits

limx→a​f(x)=L means: for every ϵ>0 there is δ>0 with ∣f(x)−L∣<ϵ whenever 0<∣x−a∣<δ.

  • Limits respect sums, products, quotients (nonzero denominator) and continuous functions: limg(f(x))=g(limf(x)) for continuous g.
  • Squeeze: if g≤f≤h near a and g,h→L, then f→L.
  • One-sided limits: limx→a​f exists iff both one-sided limits exist and agree.

3. Continuity

f is continuous at a if limx→a​f(x)=f(a).

  • Sums, products, quotients and compositions of continuous functions are continuous.
  • A piecewise function is continuous at a break point iff the two one-sided limits equal the value there.
  • Types of discontinuity: removable (limit exists, wrong value), jump (one-sided limits differ), essential (a one-sided limit does not exist, e.g. sinx1​ at 0).
  • xpsinx1​ (with value 0 at 0) is continuous at 0 iff p>0, differentiable iff p>1.

Intermediate value theorem. If f is continuous on [a,b] and y lies between f(a) and f(b), then f(c)=y for some c∈[a,b]. Use it to show roots exist, and fixed points (g(x)=f(x)−x changes sign).

Extreme value theorem. A continuous function on [a,b] attains a maximum and a minimum.

5. Applications of derivatives

  • Monotonicity: f′>0 on an interval ⇒ strictly increasing.
  • Critical points: interior extrema have f′=0 (or f′ undefined). Second derivative test: f′′>0 minimum, f′′<0 maximum.
  • Global extrema on [a,b]: compare the values at critical points and endpoints.
  • Rolle's theorem: f(a)=f(b) ⇒ f′(c)=0 for some c∈(a,b). Between two roots of f lies a root of f′; so f has at most one more root than f′.
  • Mean value theorem: f(b)−f(a)=f′(c)(b−a). Gives inequalities like ∣sinx−siny∣≤∣x−y∣ and 1+b2b−a​<arctanb−arctana<1+a2b−a​.
  • Counting real roots: find intervals of monotonicity, then check signs at the turning points and at ±∞.
  • Inequalities: to prove f≥g, show h=f−g has h(a)≥0 and h′≥0 for x≥a; e.g. ex≥1+x, log(1+x)≤x, sinx≤x for x≥0.
  • Optimisation word problems: write the quantity as a function of one variable, use the constraint, then find critical points.
  • Curve sketching: domain, intercepts, symmetry, asymptotes, monotonicity, convexity (f′′>0 convex), inflection points.
