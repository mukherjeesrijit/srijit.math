Combinatorics

1. Basic counting

  • Multiplication and addition principles: independent choices multiply; disjoint cases add.
  • Arrangements: n! orders; (n−k)!n!​ ordered choices of k; (kn​) unordered; k1​!⋯kr​!n!​ arrangements with repeated items.
  • Functions A→B with ∣A∣=m, ∣B∣=n: nm in all, (n−m)!n!​ one-to-one.
  • Fixed order: if some items must appear in a fixed relative order, choose their positions ((kn​)) and place the rest.
  • Arithmetic progressions a,a+d,… inside {1,…,N}: count the starting points for each d.
  • Involutions (f(f(x))=x) are products of disjoint swaps: ∑k​2kk!(n−2k)!n!​.

2. Advanced counting techniques

  • Stars and bars: non-negative solutions of x1​+⋯+xk​=n: (k−1n+k−1​); positive ones (k−1n−1​).
  • Gaps: increasing f(1)<⋯<f(k) with gaps ≥g become strictly increasing after subtracting (g−1)(i−1).
  • Inclusion–exclusion: ∣A1​∪⋯∪An​∣=∑∣Ai​∣−∑∣Ai​∩Aj​∣+⋯. Derangements Dn​=n!∑k=0n​k!(−1)k​.
  • Complementary counting: count what you don't want and subtract.
  • Catalan numbers Cn​=n+11​(n2n​): balanced brackets, paths that never go below the diagonal (1,1,2,5,14).
  • Alternating permutations of 1,…,5: 16 of each up-down type.

3. Binomial theorem

  • (x+y)n=∑k​(kn​)xn−kyk; the general term is (kn​)xn−kyk.
  • Identities: ∑k​(kn​)=2n, ∑k​(−1)k(kn​)=0, ∑k​k(kn​)=n2n−1, ∑k​(kn​)2=(n2n​), k+11​(kn​)=n+11​(k+1n+1​).
  • Pascal: (kn​)+(k+1n​)=(k+1n+1​).
  • Roots of unity filter: sums like ∑(−1)k(kn​)cos3kπ​ are real parts of (1−eiπ/3)n.
  • Last digits: (1+400)50=1+50⋅400+(250​)4002+⋯.

4. Pigeonhole, invariants & monovariants

  • Pigeonhole: n+1 objects in n boxes put two in one box; kn+1 objects put k+1 in one.
  • Choosing boxes: residues mod m, sub-squares of a grid, points of F32​ (any 5 contain a line, three points adding to 0).
  • Covering arguments: if m points are pairwise farther apart than the diameter of a piece, you need m pieces.
  • Invariants: parity, sums mod m, colourings. A product ∏(j−σ(j)) over an odd n is even, since ∑(j−σ(j))=0 forces an even factor.
  • Orthogonal ±1 columns: counting the four sign patterns of three columns shows 4∣n.

5. Recurrence relations

  • Set up by the first or last step: tilings with 1×1 (2 colours) and 1×2 tiles give tn​=2tn−1​+tn−2​; strings with no two consecutive 2's give Fibonacci numbers.
  • Linear recurrences an​=pan−1​+qan−2​: solve x2=px+q; with roots r=s, an​=Arn+Bsn.
  • Splitting in the middle gives identities like t2n+1​=tn​(tn−1​+tn+1​).
  • Functional equations on Z: f(x)f(1)=f(x+1)+f(x−1) is a linear recurrence.

6. Graph theory & probability

  • Graphs: ∑degv=2∣E∣; a tree on n vertices has n−1 edges; an Eulerian circuit exists iff the graph is connected and all degrees are even.
  • Extremal arguments: start from the vertex with the most neighbours of some kind.
  • Tournaments: k teams play (2k​) games among themselves, which bounds the k-th best score.
  • Classical probability: favourable over total for equally likely outcomes; P(at least one)=1−P(none).
  • Binomial and Poisson: P(X=k)=(kn​)pk(1−p)n−k; for large n and np→λ it tends to Poisson(λ).
  • Expectation by indicators: E[∑1Ai​​]=∑P(Ai​).
