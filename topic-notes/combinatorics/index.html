Combinatorics

1. Basic counting

  • Multiplication and addition principles: independent choices multiply; disjoint cases add.
  • Arrangements: $n!$ orders; $\frac{n!}{(n - k)!}$ ordered choices of $k$; $\binom nk$ unordered; $\frac{n!}{k_1!\cdots k_r!}$ arrangements with repeated items.
  • Functions $A \to B$ with $|A| = m$, $|B| = n$: $n^m$ in all, $\frac{n!}{(n - m)!}$ one-to-one.
  • Fixed order: if some items must appear in a fixed relative order, choose their positions ($\binom nk$) and place the rest.
  • Arithmetic progressions $a, a + d, \dots$ inside $\{1, \dots, N\}$: count the starting points for each $d$.
  • Involutions ($f(f(x)) = x$) are products of disjoint swaps: $\sum_k\frac{n!}{2^kk!(n - 2k)!}$.

2. Advanced counting techniques

  • Stars and bars: non-negative solutions of $x_1 + \dots + x_k = n$: $\binom{n + k - 1}{k - 1}$; positive ones $\binom{n - 1}{k - 1}$.
  • Gaps: increasing $f(1) < \dots < f(k)$ with gaps $\ge g$ become strictly increasing after subtracting $(g - 1)(i - 1)$.
  • Inclusion–exclusion: $|A_1 \cup \dots \cup A_n| = \sum|A_i| - \sum|A_i \cap A_j| + \cdots$. Derangements $D_n = n!\sum_{k=0}^n\frac{(-1)^k}{k!}$.
  • Complementary counting: count what you don't want and subtract.
  • Catalan numbers $C_n = \frac{1}{n + 1}\binom{2n}{n}$: balanced brackets, paths that never go below the diagonal ($1, 1, 2, 5, 14$).
  • Alternating permutations of $1, \dots, 5$: $16$ of each up-down type.

3. Binomial theorem

  • $(x + y)^n = \sum_k\binom nkx^{n-k}y^k$; the general term is $\binom nkx^{n-k}y^k$.
  • Identities: $\sum_k\binom nk = 2^n$, $\sum_k(-1)^k\binom nk = 0$, $\sum_kk\binom nk = n2^{n-1}$, $\sum_k\binom nk^2 = \binom{2n}{n}$, $\frac{1}{k+1}\binom nk = \frac{1}{n+1}\binom{n+1}{k+1}$.
  • Pascal: $\binom nk + \binom n{k+1} = \binom{n+1}{k+1}$.
  • Roots of unity filter: sums like $\sum(-1)^k\binom nk\cos\frac{k\pi}{3}$ are real parts of $(1 - e^{i\pi/3})^n$.
  • Last digits: $(1 + 400)^{50} = 1 + 50\cdot400 + \binom{50}{2}400^2 + \cdots$.

4. Pigeonhole, invariants & monovariants

  • Pigeonhole: $n + 1$ objects in $n$ boxes put two in one box; $kn + 1$ objects put $k + 1$ in one.
  • Choosing boxes: residues mod $m$, sub-squares of a grid, points of $\mathbb{F}_3^2$ (any $5$ contain a line, three points adding to $0$).
  • Covering arguments: if $m$ points are pairwise farther apart than the diameter of a piece, you need $m$ pieces.
  • Invariants: parity, sums mod $m$, colourings. A product $\prod(j - \sigma(j))$ over an odd $n$ is even, since $\sum(j - \sigma(j)) = 0$ forces an even factor.
  • Orthogonal $\pm1$ columns: counting the four sign patterns of three columns shows $4 \mid n$.

5. Recurrence relations

  • Set up by the first or last step: tilings with $1 \times 1$ (2 colours) and $1 \times 2$ tiles give $t_n = 2t_{n-1} + t_{n-2}$; strings with no two consecutive 2's give Fibonacci numbers.
  • Linear recurrences $a_n = pa_{n-1} + qa_{n-2}$: solve $x^2 = px + q$; with roots $r \ne s$, $a_n = Ar^n + Bs^n$.
  • Splitting in the middle gives identities like $t_{2n+1} = t_n(t_{n-1} + t_{n+1})$.
  • Functional equations on $\mathbb{Z}$: $f(x)f(1) = f(x + 1) + f(x - 1)$ is a linear recurrence.

6. Graph theory & probability

  • Graphs: $\sum\deg v = 2|E|$; a tree on $n$ vertices has $n - 1$ edges; an Eulerian circuit exists iff the graph is connected and all degrees are even.
  • Extremal arguments: start from the vertex with the most neighbours of some kind.
  • Tournaments: $k$ teams play $\binom k2$ games among themselves, which bounds the $k$-th best score.
  • Classical probability: favourable over total for equally likely outcomes; $P(\text{at least one}) = 1 - P(\text{none})$.
  • Binomial and Poisson: $P(X = k) = \binom nkp^k(1 - p)^{n-k}$; for large $n$ and $np \to \lambda$ it tends to Poisson$(\lambda)$.
  • Expectation by indicators: $E[\sum\mathbf{1}_{A_i}] = \sum P(A_i)$.
