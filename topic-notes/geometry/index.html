Geometry

1. Triangles

  • Angles: they sum to π; an exterior angle equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles.
  • Congruence: SSS, SAS, ASA, RHS. Similarity: AA, SAS, SSS in proportion; areas scale by the square of the ratio.
  • Pythagoras: a2+b2=c2 iff the angle opposite c is right. A right triangle has inradius 2a+b−c​ and circumradius 2c​.
  • Law of cosines c2=a2+b2−2abcosC; law of sines sinAa​=2R.
  • Area: 21​absinC=s(s−a)(s−b)(s−c)​=rs=4Rabc​.
  • Cevians: median length ma2​=42b2+2c2−a2​; angle bisector divides the opposite side as ACAB​; a cevian splits the area in the ratio of the bases.
  • Area ratios with cevians: triangles with a common vertex and bases on one line have areas in the ratio of the bases.
  • Centres: centroid (medians, 2:1), incentre (angle bisectors), circumcentre (perpendicular bisectors), orthocentre (altitudes). In an acute triangle the feet of the altitudes form the orthic triangle, which the altitudes bisect.
  • Two ladders crossing between walls of heights a and b meet at height a+bab​.

2. Polygons & quadrilaterals

  • Interior angles of an n-gon sum to (n−2)π; each exterior angle of a regular n-gon is n2π​.
  • A regular n-gon inscribed in radius r has area 2n​r2sinn2π​.
  • Parallelogram: diagonals bisect each other; ABCD is a parallelogram iff A+C=B+D (as vectors).
  • Quadrilateral area: 21​d1​d2​sinθ with diagonals d1​,d2​ at angle θ.
  • Squares on the sides: the triangle between squares on two sides at a vertex has the same area as the triangle formed by those two sides.
  • Extremal polygons: among n-gons inscribed in a circle the regular one has the largest area (Jensen for sin).

3. Circles

  • Angles: the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference; angles in the same segment are equal; the angle in a semicircle is right.
  • Cyclic quadrilateral: opposite angles add to π.
  • Tangents are perpendicular to the radius; the two tangents from a point are equal.
  • Power of a point: PA⋅PB=PC⋅PD for chords or secants through P, and =PT2 for a tangent.
  • Chords: equal chords are equidistant from the centre; a chord at distance d from the centre has length 2r2−d2​.
  • Crossing chords PQ, RS at O: triangles POR and SOQ are similar.

4. Area & volume

  • Circle πr2, sector 21​r2θ, segment 21​r2(θ−sinθ).
  • Prism Bh, pyramid and cone 31​Bh, cylinder πr2h, sphere 34​πr3 (surface 4πr2), frustum 3h​(B1​+B1​B2​​+B2​).
  • A point inside a square: the triangles on opposite sides have areas adding to half the square.
  • Cross-sections: a plane through the axis of a cone gives an isosceles triangle; through two apexes of a double cone, a rhombus.

5. Coordinate & transformational geometry

  • Distance (x1​−x2​)2+(y1​−y2​)2​; section formula m+nmx2​+nx1​​; area of a triangle 21​∣x1​(y2​−y3​)+x2​(y3​−y1​)+x3​(y1​−y2​)∣.
  • Lines: slopes m1​m2​=−1 for perpendicular lines; distance from (x0​,y0​) to ax+by+c=0 is a2+b2​∣ax0​+by0​+c∣​.
  • Reflection of P in a line: the foot F of the perpendicular, then P′=2F−P.
  • Circles: x2+y2+2gx+2fy+c=0 has centre (−g,−f) and radius g2+f2−c​. Two circles: subtracting the equations gives the common chord; the number of common tangents is 4,3,2,1,0 as d>r1​+r2​, =, between, =∣r1​−r2​∣, less.
  • Loci: write the point in terms of a parameter and eliminate it.
  • Transformations: translations and rotations keep distances and areas; a dilation by k multiplies areas by k2.
