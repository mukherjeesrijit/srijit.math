Number Theory

1. Divisibility & primes

  • a∣b and a∣c give a∣bx+cy. Fundamental theorem: every n>1 is a product of primes in one way.
  • Tests: 3,9 by digit sums; 11 by alternating digit sums; 4,8 by the last two, three digits; 72=8⋅9 by both.
  • gcd(a,b)lcm(a,b)=ab. Divisible by 9 and 15 means divisible by 45.
  • Products of primes: p1​p=(m−2)(m+2)-type equations force the factors to be 1,p1​,p or p1​p.
  • Primes in a window: among 30 consecutive integers above 5, at most φ(30)=8 are prime; of three numbers p−4,p,p+4 one is divisible by 3.
  • Sums of consecutive integers: n is such a sum (of at least two) iff n is not a power of 2.
  • n! is divisible by every prime ≤n, and by every number up to n.

2. Euclidean algorithm & Diophantine equations

  • gcd(a,b)=gcd(b,amodb). Bézout: ax+by=gcd(a,b) has integer solutions; ax+by=c is solvable iff gcd(a,b)∣c, and then x=x0​+db​t, y=y0​−da​t.
  • Factor the equation: x2−y2=(x−y)(x+y); (x−2)(y−2)=2 from xy−2x−2y+2=0.
  • Pythagorean triples: primitive ones are (m2−n2,2mn,m2+n2) with gcd(m,n)=1, m−n odd.
  • Bounding: for a1​+b1​+c1​=… with a<b<c, a large a makes the left side too small.
  • Rational points: lines through a known point of a conic meet it again at rational points.

3. Modular arithmetic

  • a≡b(modm) iff m∣a−b; congruences can be added and multiplied. a has an inverse mod m iff gcd(a,m)=1.
  • Powers cycle: compute akmodm until it repeats (270+370, 40150 mod powers of 10).
  • Linear combinations: to find 11x+5ymod7 from 4x+3y and 2x+4y, write it as a combination of them.
  • Polynomials mod p: check every residue 0,…,p−1.
  • Same remainder: if a,b,c leave the same remainder mod N, then N divides the differences.

4. Fermat, Euler, Wilson & CRT

  • Fermat: ap−1≡1(modp) for p∤a. Euler: aφ(n)≡1(modn) for gcd(a,n)=1. Wilson: (p−1)!≡−1(modp).
  • CRT: x≡a(modm), x≡b(modn) with gcd(m,n)=1 has one solution mod mn; with d=gcd(m,n) it is solvable iff a≡b(modd), and then unique mod lcm(m,n).
  • Sums of powers: ∑k=1p−1​km≡0(modp) unless (p−1)∣m.

5. Arithmetic functions

  • For n=∏piai​​: number of divisors τ(n)=∏(ai​+1), sum σ(n)=∏pi​−1piai​+1​−1​, φ(n)=n∏(1−pi​1​).
  • Product of the divisors: nτ(n)/2.
  • Given the number of divisors: factor τ(n) to list the exponent patterns (2023=7⋅17⋅17).
  • Divisors by residue: divisors ≡1 or 2(mod3) of 5k alternate, since 5≡−1.
  • Multiplicative maps: if 1−f(xy)=(1−f(x))(1−f(y)), then g=1−f is multiplicative.

6. Bases & digits

  • n=∑di​10i; n≡ digit sum mod 9. A number has ⌊log10​n⌋+1 digits.
  • Product of digits g(n)≤n (compare with the leading term).
  • Binary fractions: 0.1875=163​=0.00112​.
  • Factorial base: every positive rational is uniquely a1​+2!a2​​+⋯+n!an​​ with 0≤ak​<k.
  • Unknown digits (x679y divisible by 72): use the divisibility tests one at a time.
