Number Theory

1. Divisibility & primes

  • $a \mid b$ and $a \mid c$ give $a \mid bx + cy$. Fundamental theorem: every $n > 1$ is a product of primes in one way.
  • Tests: $3, 9$ by digit sums; $11$ by alternating digit sums; $4, 8$ by the last two, three digits; $72 = 8\cdot9$ by both.
  • $\gcd(a, b)\,\mathrm{lcm}(a, b) = ab$. Divisible by $9$ and $15$ means divisible by $45$.
  • Products of primes: $p_1p = (m - 2)(m + 2)$-type equations force the factors to be $1, p_1, p$ or $p_1p$.
  • Primes in a window: among $30$ consecutive integers above $5$, at most $\varphi(30) = 8$ are prime; of three numbers $p - 4, p, p + 4$ one is divisible by $3$.
  • Sums of consecutive integers: $n$ is such a sum (of at least two) iff $n$ is not a power of $2$.
  • $n!$ is divisible by every prime $\le n$, and by every number up to $n$.

2. Euclidean algorithm & Diophantine equations

  • $\gcd(a, b) = \gcd(b, a \bmod b)$. Bézout: $ax + by = \gcd(a, b)$ has integer solutions; $ax + by = c$ is solvable iff $\gcd(a, b) \mid c$, and then $x = x_0 + \frac{b}{d}t$, $y = y_0 - \frac adt$.
  • Factor the equation: $x^2 - y^2 = (x - y)(x + y)$; $(x - 2)(y - 2) = 2$ from $xy - 2x - 2y + 2 = 0$.
  • Pythagorean triples: primitive ones are $(m^2 - n^2, 2mn, m^2 + n^2)$ with $\gcd(m, n) = 1$, $m - n$ odd.
  • Bounding: for $\frac1a + \frac1b + \frac1c = \dots$ with $a < b < c$, a large $a$ makes the left side too small.
  • Rational points: lines through a known point of a conic meet it again at rational points.

3. Modular arithmetic

  • $a \equiv b \pmod m$ iff $m \mid a - b$; congruences can be added and multiplied. $a$ has an inverse mod $m$ iff $\gcd(a, m) = 1$.
  • Powers cycle: compute $a^k \bmod m$ until it repeats ($2^{70} + 3^{70}$, $401^{50}$ mod powers of $10$).
  • Linear combinations: to find $11x + 5y \bmod 7$ from $4x + 3y$ and $2x + 4y$, write it as a combination of them.
  • Polynomials mod $p$: check every residue $0, \dots, p - 1$.
  • Same remainder: if $a, b, c$ leave the same remainder mod $N$, then $N$ divides the differences.

4. Fermat, Euler, Wilson & CRT

  • Fermat: $a^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod p$ for $p \nmid a$. Euler: $a^{\varphi(n)} \equiv 1 \pmod n$ for $\gcd(a, n) = 1$. Wilson: $(p - 1)! \equiv -1 \pmod p$.
  • CRT: $x \equiv a \pmod m$, $x \equiv b \pmod n$ with $\gcd(m, n) = 1$ has one solution mod $mn$; with $d = \gcd(m, n)$ it is solvable iff $a \equiv b \pmod d$, and then unique mod $\mathrm{lcm}(m, n)$.
  • Sums of powers: $\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}k^{m} \equiv 0 \pmod p$ unless $(p - 1) \mid m$.

5. Arithmetic functions

  • For $n = \prod p_i^{a_i}$: number of divisors $\tau(n) = \prod(a_i + 1)$, sum $\sigma(n) = \prod\frac{p_i^{a_i+1} - 1}{p_i - 1}$, $\varphi(n) = n\prod(1 - \frac1{p_i})$.
  • Product of the divisors: $n^{\tau(n)/2}$.
  • Given the number of divisors: factor $\tau(n)$ to list the exponent patterns ($2023 = 7\cdot17\cdot17$).
  • Divisors by residue: divisors $\equiv 1$ or $2 \pmod 3$ of $5^k$ alternate, since $5 \equiv -1$.
  • Multiplicative maps: if $1 - f(xy) = (1 - f(x))(1 - f(y))$, then $g = 1 - f$ is multiplicative.

6. Bases & digits

  • $n = \sum d_i10^i$; $n \equiv$ digit sum mod $9$. A number has $\lfloor\log_{10}n\rfloor + 1$ digits.
  • Product of digits $g(n) \le n$ (compare with the leading term).
  • Binary fractions: $0.1875 = \frac3{16} = 0.0011_2$.
  • Factorial base: every positive rational is uniquely $a_1 + \frac{a_2}{2!} + \dots + \frac{a_n}{n!}$ with $0 \le a_k < k$.
  • Unknown digits ($x679y$ divisible by $72$): use the divisibility tests one at a time.
