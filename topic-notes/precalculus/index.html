Precalculus

1. Advanced functions

  • One-to-one and onto: injective if f(a)=f(b)⇒a=b; surjective if every value is attained. A strictly monotone function is injective.
  • Composition and inverses: f−1 exists iff f is a bijection; the graph of f−1 is the reflection in y=x. An involution (f∘f=id) is its own inverse.
  • Fractional part {x}=x−⌊x⌋; x↦{x+21​} shifts [0,1) cyclically.
  • Symmetry tricks: if f(x)+f(1−x)=1, pair the terms of a sum (f(x)=1+e2x−1e2x−1​).
  • Even and odd parts: f(x)=2f(x)+f(−x)​+2f(x)−f(−x)​.
  • Images of sets: f(A∩B)⊆f(A)∩f(B), with equality for all A,B iff f is injective.

2. Polynomial & rational analysis

  • A degree-n polynomial has at most n real roots and at most n−1 turning points; an odd degree polynomial takes every real value.
  • Multiplicity: near a root of multiplicity m, p(x)≈c(x−a)m: it crosses the axis iff m is odd, and p(x)(x−a)p′(x)​→m.
  • Counting roots in an interval: signs at the ends plus monotonicity from p′.
  • Asymptotes of QP​: vertical where Q=0 (and P=0), horizontal from the leading terms when degP≤degQ, slant when degP=degQ+1.

3. Exponentials & logarithms

  • loga​ is increasing for a>1 and decreasing for 0<a<1; loga​f is defined only where f>0.
  • loga​(xy)=loga​x+loga​y, loga​b=lnalnb​, aloga​b=b, nlogm=mlogn.
  • Equations like cx=q(x): compare ranges (cx>0 against the sign of q).
  • Growth: ex beats every power of x, which beats every power of logx.

4. Trigonometry

  • sin2+cos2=1; sin(A±B), cos(A±B); sin2θ=2sinθcosθ; cos3θ=4cos3θ−3cosθ, sin3θ=3sinθ−4sin3θ.
  • Products: 4sinθsin(3π​+θ)sin(3π​−θ)=sin3θ.
  • asinx+bcosx=a2+b2​sin(x+φ), with range [−a2+b2​,a2+b2​].
  • Half angles: 21+cosθ​​=cos2θ​; nested radicals 2+2+⋯​​=2cos2n+1π​.
  • Periods: sin(ax+b) has period ∣a∣2π​; ∣sinx∣+∣cosx∣ has period 2π​.
  • Equations: bring to one function, or divide by cos2x to get a quadratic in tanx; check that both sides can meet (e.g. 2cos(⋅)≤2≤2x+2−x).
  • In a triangle: cos2A+cos2B+cos2C+2cosAcosBcosC=1.

5. Complex numbers & polar coordinates

6. Vectors & matrices

  • Dot product u⋅v=∣u∣∣v∣cosθ; cross product ∣u×v∣=∣u∣∣v∣sinθ, perpendicular to both.
  • Lines and planes: a plane through a point with normal n is n⋅(x−x0​)=0; planes through the line of intersection of P1​=0 and P2​=0 are P1​+λP2​=0.
  • Determinants: det(AB)=detAdetB, det(An)=(detA)n, det(kA)=kndetA; row operations and expansion along a row; a matrix is invertible iff det=0.

  • Rotations: (cosθsinθ​−sinθcosθ​)n is rotation by nθ.

7. Conic sections, induction & limits

  • Parabola y2=4ax: tangent at (at2,2at) is ty=x+at2; chord of contact from (h,k) is ky=2a(x+h).
  • Ellipse a2x2​+b2y2​=1: y=mx+c is tangent iff c2=a2m2+b2. Hyperbola a2x2​−b2y2​=1: c2=a2m2−b2.
  • Classifying ax2+2hxy+by2+⋯=0: ellipse, parabola or hyperbola as h2−ab<,=,>0 (or degenerate: a point, lines).
  • Confocal conics a2+λx2​+b2+λy2​=1: eliminate λ by differentiating.
  • Induction: prove P(1) and P(n)⇒P(n+1); strong induction assumes all smaller cases.
  • Limits: xsinx​→1, (1+na​)n→ea; a limit of cosn(n−x) is found from nlogcos(n−x)≈−2n1−2x​.
