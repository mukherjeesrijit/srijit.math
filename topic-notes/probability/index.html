1. Counting and elementary probability

  • Permutations and combinations: n! arrangements; (kn​) choices; multinomial k1​!⋯kr​!n!​.

Conditional probability and independence

  • P(A∣B)=P(B)P(A∩B)​; multiplication rule P(A∩B)=P(B)P(A∣B).
  • Total probability: P(A)=∑P(A∣Bi​)P(Bi​) for a partition {Bi​}.

  • A,B independent iff P(A∩B)=P(A)P(B). Pairwise independence does not imply mutual independence.

2. Random variables and expectation

  • CDF: F(x)=P(X≤x): nondecreasing, right-continuous, F(−∞)=0, F(∞)=1. P(a<X≤b)=F(b)−F(a).
  • Discrete: pmf p(x); continuous: density f=F′ with P(a<X<b)=∫ab​f.
  • Expectation: E[g(X)]=∑g(x)p(x) or ∫g(x)f(x)dx. Linear: E[aX+bY]=aE[X]+bE[Y] always.
  • Tail formula: for X≥0, E[X]=∫0∞​P(X>t)dt; for integer X≥0, E[X]=∑k≥1​P(X≥k).
  • Variance: VarX=E[X2]−(EX)2, Var(aX+b)=a2VarX. E(X−c)2=VarX+(EX−c)2.
  • Indicators: X=∑1Ai​​ gives E[X]=∑P(Ai​). (Expected fixed points of a random permutation: 1.)
  • Moment generating function: M(t)=E[etX]; M(k)(0)=E[Xk]; it determines the distribution.

Inequalities

  • Markov: P(X≥a)≤aE[X]​ for X≥0.
  • Chebyshev: P(∣X−μ∣≥k)≤k2σ2​, so P(∣X−μ∣<k)≥1−k2σ2​.

4. Joint distributions

  • Joint density f(x,y); marginal fX​(x)=∫f(x,y)dy; conditional fY∣X​(y∣x)=fX​(x)f(x,y)​.
  • Independence: f(x,y)=fX​(x)fY​(y) (the support must be a product set).
  • Covariance: Cov(X,Y)=E[XY]−E[X]E[Y]; Var(X+Y)=VarX+VarY+2Cov(X,Y); correlation ρ∈[−1,1]. Independent ⇒ uncorrelated, not conversely (except jointly normal).
  • Conditional expectation: E[E[Y∣X]]=E[Y]; VarY=E[Var(Y∣X)]+Var(E[Y∣X]).
  • Sums: the density of X+Y (independent) is the convolution ∫fX​(t)fY​(z−t)dt. Sums of independent Poissons, normals, gammas (same rate) stay in the family.
  • Order statistics of an i.i.d. sample with CDF F: P(X(n)​≤x)=F(x)n, P(X(1)​>x)=(1−F(x))n. The minimum of n i.i.d. Exp(λ) is Exp(nλ).
  • Change of variables: fU,V​(u,v)=fX,Y​(x,y)​det∂(u,v)∂(x,y)​​.

Multivariate normal and sampling distributions

  • Linear combinations of a jointly normal vector are normal; for jointly normal variables, uncorrelated means independent.

5. Limit theorems

  • Convergence in probability: P(∣Xn​−X∣>ϵ)→0. In distribution: Fn​(x)→F(x) at continuity points of F.
  • Weak law of large numbers: for i.i.d. with finite mean μ, Xˉn​→μ in probability (Chebyshev gives it when the variance is finite). So n1​∑g(Xi​)→E[g(X)].
  • Central limit theorem: for i.i.d. with mean μ and variance σ2, σn​(Xˉn​−μ)​→N(0,1) in distribution. E.g. for Exp(1) sums, P(Sn​>n)→21​; for χn2​, P(χn2​>n+2n​)→P(Z>1)≈0.1587.
  • Continuous mapping: if Xn​→X then g(Xn​)→g(X) for continuous g. Slutsky: if Xn​→X in distribution and Yn​→c, then Xn​+Yn​→X+c and Xn​Yn​→cX.
  • Delta method: n​(g(Xˉn​)−g(μ))→N(0,g′(μ)2σ2).
