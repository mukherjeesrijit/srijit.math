Quantitative Finance

Probability for quant interviews: short problems, a clean idea each. Most answers come from one of a few tricks: count carefully, condition on the first step, use linearity of expectation, or find a symmetry.

1. Counting & classical probability

  • Equally likely outcomes: $P(A) = \frac{|A|}{|\Omega|}$. Decide first whether order matters and whether repeats are allowed.
  • Lattice paths: from $(0, 0)$ to $(m, n)$ with unit steps right and up: $\binom{m+n}{m}$. Through a point: multiply; avoiding a point: subtract.
  • Dice sums: count solutions of $d_1 + \dots + d_k = s$ with $1 \le d_i \le 6$ by stars and bars, removing cases with $d_i \ge 7$ (inclusion–exclusion), or use symmetry $d_i \mapsto 7 - d_i$.
  • Increasing order: for distinct values, each of the $k!$ orders is equally likely.
  • Circular arrangements: $n$ people around a table up to rotation: $(n - 1)!$.
  • Digits: count by place value; each place takes each digit equally often.

2. Conditional probability & Bayes

  • $P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}$. Bayes: $P(D \mid +) = \frac{P(+ \mid D)P(D)}{P(+ \mid D)P(D) + P(+ \mid D^c)P(D^c)}$; with a rare disease, most positives are false.
  • Total probability: condition on what happens first (which cherry moved, which box was chosen).
  • "A before B": in repeated independent trials only $A$ and $B$ matter: $P(A \text{ first}) = \frac{P(A)}{P(A) + P(B)}$.
  • Conditioning traps: "at least one is a head" is not "the first is a head". Given that the first $5$-or-$6$ is a $6$, the waiting time is unchanged.

  • Poisson processes: counts in disjoint intervals are independent Poisson; given $n$ arrivals in a period, their times are i.i.d. uniform.
  • Binomial$(n, \frac{\lambda}{n}) \to$ Poisson$(\lambda)$: e.g. fixed points of a random function.

4. Continuous distributions

  • Uniform$(a, b)$: mean $\frac{a+b}{2}$, variance $\frac{(b-a)^2}{12}$. Exponential$(\lambda)$: mean $\frac1\lambda$, memoryless. Normal: symmetric, so $P(Y > cX) = \frac12$ for independent standard normals.
  • Geometric probability: a uniform point in a region: probability $=$ area ratio (the bomb in a circle, breaking a stick: $\frac14$).
  • Order statistics of uniforms: the $k$-th smallest of $n$ i.i.d. Uniform$(0, 1)$ has mean $\frac{k}{n+1}$; $n$ points cut $[0, 1]$ (or a circle) into pieces of equal expected length.
  • Odd moments of $N(0, 1)$ vanish: $\mathrm{Cov}(Z, Z^2) = 0$.

5. Multivariate distributions

  • $\mathrm{Cov}(X, Y) = E[XY] - E[X]E[Y]$; $\mathrm{Var}(X + Y) = \mathrm{Var}X + \mathrm{Var}Y + 2\mathrm{Cov}(X, Y)$.
  • Correlation $\rho = \frac{\mathrm{Cov}}{\sigma_X\sigma_Y} \in [-1, 1]$; linear maps $a + bX$ change only its sign (sign of $b$); $|\mathrm{Cov}| \le \sigma_X\sigma_Y$.
  • Bivariate normal: $E[Y \mid X = x] = \mu_Y + \frac{\mathrm{Cov}(X, Y)}{\mathrm{Var}X}(x - \mu_X)$.
  • Brownian motion: $\mathrm{Cov}(W_s, W_t) = \min(s, t)$; the bridge $W_t - tW_1$ has covariance $s(1 - t)$ for $s \le t$.

6. Expectation & random variables

  • Linearity works without independence: expected fixed points, adjacent couples ($n\cdot\frac{2}{2n-1}$), complete sets left.
  • Tail sums: for $X \ge 0$ integer, $E[X] = \sum_{k\ge1}P(X \ge k)$; e.g. the minimum of dice.
  • Max and min: $P(\max \le n) = \left(\frac{n}{6}\right)^2$ for two dice; then $P(\max = n) = \frac{2n-1}{36}$.
  • First-step analysis: write the expected value (or waiting time) from each state and solve the linear equations.
  • Optimal stopping: stop when the current reward beats the expected value of continuing; work backwards from the last decision.

7. Limit theorems

  • Markov $P(X \ge a) \le \frac{E X}{a}$; Chebyshev $P(|X - \mu| \ge k) \le \frac{\sigma^2}{k^2}$; Jensen $E[\varphi(X)] \ge \varphi(E X)$ for convex $\varphi$ (so $M(4) \ge M(1)^4$).
  • CLT: a sum of $n$ i.i.d. terms is approximately $N(n\mu, n\sigma^2)$: $100$ fair tosses have $50 \pm 5$ heads, and $60$ is $2$ standard deviations up ($\approx 2.3\%$).
  • Derangements: $P_n = \sum_{k=0}^n\frac{(-1)^k}{k!} \to \frac1e$, extremely fast.

8. Brainteasers, games & random walks

  • Divisors: a number has an odd number of divisors iff it is a perfect square.
  • Take-away games: find the losing positions (multiples of $k + 1$ when you may take $1$ to $k$) and move to them.
  • Symmetry and pairing: answer what the opponent does with the complementary move.
  • Information puzzles: a designated counter turns a hard puzzle into counting.
  • Random walks: the probability of reaching $a$ before $-b$ (fair walk from $0$) is $\frac{b}{a+b}$; expected time is $ab$.
