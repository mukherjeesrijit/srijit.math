Real Analysis

1. Sets, functions and countability

  • Images and preimages: f(∪Si​)=∪f(Si​) and f(∩Si​)⊆∩f(Si​) (equality when f is injective). Preimages preserve unions, intersections and complements.
  • Countable: finite or in bijection with N. Z, Q, N×N, finite subsets of N, algebraic numbers: countable. A countable union of countable sets is countable.
  • Uncountable: R, any interval, {0,1}N, the Cantor set. (Cantor's diagonal argument.)
  • Useful facts: a set of disjoint open intervals is countable (each holds a rational); if ∑x∈E​f(x)≤1 for all finite E then {f>0} is countable; the discontinuities of a monotone function are countable.
  • Binary/decimal expansions: every x∈[0,1] has a binary expansion; dyadic rationals have two. The map {0,1}N→[0,1], (an​)↦∑an​2−n, is onto but not one-one; with base 10 and digits 0,1 it is one-one but not onto.

2. Sequences

  • an​→L: for every ϵ>0 there is N with ∣an​−L∣<ϵ for n≥N. Convergent sequences are bounded.
  • Monotone convergence: a bounded monotone sequence converges (to its sup or inf). For recursions an+1​=g(an​): show boundedness and monotonicity by induction, then the limit solves L=g(L).
  • Bolzano–Weierstrass: every bounded sequence has a convergent subsequence.
  • Cauchy: ∣am​−an​∣<ϵ for m,n≥N. In R, Cauchy ⟺ convergent. Contractions ∣an+1​−an​∣≤c∣an​−an−1​∣ (c<1) give Cauchy sequences.
  • Limsup/liminf: the largest and smallest subsequential limits; an​→L iff limsup=liminf=L. For continuous f: liminff(xn​)≤f(liminfxn​) need not be equality.
  • Standard limits: n1/n→1, a1/n→1 (a>0), (1+na​)n→ea, bnnk​→0 (b>1), (an+bn)1/n→max(a,b).
  • Cesàro: if an​→L then na1​+⋯+an​​→L. Stolz–Cesàro: if bn​↑∞ and bn+1​−bn​an+1​−an​​→L then bn​an​​→L.
  • Ratio and root: if an​an+1​​→r then an1/n​→r; r<1 gives an​→0.
  • Linear recurrences: xn+1​=axn​+bxn−1​ has solutions αr1n​+βr2n​ from r2=ar+b; it converges iff the active roots satisfy ∣r∣<1 or r=1.
  • Harmonic numbers: Hn​=logn+γ+o(1); ∑j=n+1kn​j1​→logk.

3. Series

  • ∑an​ converges iff its partial sums converge. Necessary: an​→0.
  • Geometric: ∑rn=1−r1​ for ∣r∣<1; ∑nrn=(1−r)2r​. Telescoping: ∑(bn​−bn+1​)=b1​−limbn​.
  • p-series: ∑np1​ converges iff p>1; ∑n(logn)q1​ iff q>1.
  • Comparison and limit comparison (positive terms): if bn​an​​→c∈(0,∞), they converge together.
  • Ratio test: lim​an​an+1​​​<1 converges, >1 diverges. Root test: limsup∣an​∣1/n<1 converges.
  • Integral test: for decreasing f≥0, ∑f(n) and ∫1∞​f converge together; also ∫n∞​f≤∑k≥n​f(k)≤f(n)+∫n∞​f, e.g. ∑k≥n​k21​∼n1​.
  • Cauchy condensation: for decreasing an​≥0, ∑an​ and ∑2ka2k​ converge together.
  • Alternating series (Leibniz): if bn​↓0 then ∑(−1)nbn​ converges.
  • Absolute vs conditional: absolute convergence implies convergence; rearrangements of conditionally convergent series can change the sum (Riemann).
  • Dirichlet/Abel: ∑an​bn​ converges if the partial sums of an​ are bounded and bn​↓0; or if ∑an​ converges and bn​ is monotone and bounded. So ∑an​ convergent ⇒∑nan​​ convergent.
  • Products: ∑an​bn​ can diverge when ∑an​,∑bn​ converge conditionally (an​=bn​=n​(−1)n​); it converges absolutely if one converges absolutely and the other's terms are bounded.

4. Continuity and uniform continuity

  • Sequential criterion: f is continuous at a iff xn​→a implies f(xn​)→f(a).
  • Functions defined differently on Q and R∖Q are continuous exactly where the two formulas agree (by density of both sets).
  • Uniformly continuous: one δ works for all x. Lipschitz (∣f(x)−f(y)∣≤K∣x−y∣) and Hölder (≤K∣x−y∣α) functions are uniformly continuous; x​ is uniformly continuous on [0,∞), x2 and sin(x2) on R are not.
  • Heine–Cantor: continuous on [a,b] ⇒ uniformly continuous.
  • Uniformly continuous on (a,b) ⇒ extends continuously to [a,b] (Cauchy sequences go to Cauchy sequences).
  • Uniformly continuous on R ⇒ ∣f(x)∣≤A+B∣x∣.
  • Monotone functions: one-sided limits exist everywhere; jumps are countable and their sizes add up to at most f(b)−f(a).
  • Functional equations: f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y) with continuity gives f(x)=cx; f(x+1)=f(x)+1 with continuity gives f(x)=x+(bounded periodic).
  • Fixed points: a continuous f:[a,b]→[a,b] has a fixed point. A strict contraction (∣f(x)−f(y)∣<∣x−y∣) on [a,b] has exactly one.

5. Mean value theorem and Taylor's theorem

  • Rolle, Lagrange MVT, Cauchy MVT: g(b)−g(a)f(b)−f(a)​=g′(c)f′(c)​.
  • Consequences: f′=0 on an interval ⇒ constant (on an open set: constant on each component, so countably many values); ∣f′∣≤K ⇒ K-Lipschitz; ∣f(x)−f(y)∣≤∣x−y∣1+ϵ ⇒ f constant.
  • f with n zeros has f′ with at least n−1 zeros. If f(xn​)=c at points xn​→a, then f′(a)=0 (and so on for higher derivatives by Rolle).
  • Darboux: derivatives have the intermediate value property (even when not continuous).
  • If f→L and f′→α as x→∞, then α=0. If f′′→1 then x2f(x)​→21​.
  • Taylor with Lagrange remainder: f(x)=∑k=0n​k!f(k)(a)​(x−a)k+(n+1)!f(n+1)(c)​(x−a)n+1.
  • Symmetric quotients: h2f(h)+f(−h)−2f(0)​→f′′(0).
  • Smooth but not analytic: e−1/x2 (value 0 at 0) has all derivatives 0 at 0.
  • Convexity: f′′≥0 ⟺ convex ⟺ f(tx+(1−t)y)≤tf(x)+(1−t)f(y); Jensen's inequality.

6. Riemann integration and improper integrals

  • Riemann integrable (bounded f on [a,b]): upper and lower sums can be made arbitrarily close. Continuous, monotone, and bounded functions with countably many discontinuities are integrable (Lebesgue's criterion: discontinuities of measure zero). 1Q​ is not.
  • Fundamental theorem: for integrable f, F(x)=∫ax​f is Lipschitz; F′(x)=f(x) where f is continuous.
  • Mean value theorem for integrals and weighted MVT: ∫fg=f(c)∫g for continuous f and g≥0.
  • Moments determine oddness/evenness: if ∫−11​f(x)x2ndx=0 for all n (continuous f), Weierstrass approximation gives f odd.
  • Improper integrals: ∫1∞​x−p converges iff p>1; ∫01​x−p iff p<1. Compare ∣g∣∣f∣​∼cxm−n for polynomials: ∫1∞​ converges iff n−m≥2.
  • Absolute vs conditional: ∫1∞​xsinx​ converges, ∫1∞​x∣sinx∣​ diverges.
  • Averages: if ∫nn+1​f→c then T1​∫0T​f→c (Cesàro).
  • Concentration: (n+1)∫01​xnf(x)dx→f(1); ∫01​1+n2x2n​g(x)dx→2π​g(0).

7. Sequences and series of functions

  • Pointwise: fn​(x)→f(x) for each x. Uniform: supx​∣fn​(x)−f(x)∣→0.
  • To test uniformity: compute Mn​=sup∣fn​−f∣ (calculus: maximise fn​−f). If the limit is discontinuous but the fn​ are continuous, convergence is not uniform. Moving bumps (fn​ peaking at n1​ with height not tending to 0) converge pointwise but not uniformly.
  • Uniform limits: of continuous functions are continuous; ∫fn​→∫f on [a,b]. Derivatives need more: if fn′​→g uniformly and fn​(x0​) converges, then fn​→f uniformly with f′=g.
  • Uniform convergence on (a,b) of continuous functions on [a,b] extends to [a,b].
  • Dini: monotone convergence of continuous functions to a continuous limit on a compact set is uniform. (Decreasing limits of continuous increasing functions are right-continuous.)
  • Weierstrass M-test: ∣un​(x)∣≤Mn​ with ∑Mn​<∞ ⇒ ∑un​ converges uniformly.
  • Power series ∑an​(x−c)n: radius R=limsup∣an​∣1/n1​ (or lim​an+1​an​​​). Uniform on ∣x−c∣≤r<R; differentiate and integrate term by term inside. Check the endpoints separately: ∑nxn​ converges on [−1,1), not uniformly; ∑n2xn​ converges on [−1,1] but its derivative blows up at 1.
  • Weierstrass approximation: polynomials are dense in C[a,b] (sup norm).
