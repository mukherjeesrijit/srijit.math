Real Analysis

1. Sets, functions and countability

  • Images and preimages: $f(\cup S_i)=\cup f(S_i)$ and $f(\cap S_i)\subseteq\cap f(S_i)$ (equality when $f$ is injective). Preimages preserve unions, intersections and complements.
  • Countable: finite or in bijection with $\mathbb N$. $\mathbb Z$, $\mathbb Q$, $\mathbb N\times\mathbb N$, finite subsets of $\mathbb N$, algebraic numbers: countable. A countable union of countable sets is countable.
  • Uncountable: $\mathbb R$, any interval, $\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N}$, the Cantor set. (Cantor's diagonal argument.)
  • Useful facts: a set of disjoint open intervals is countable (each holds a rational); if $\sum_{x\in E}f(x)\le1$ for all finite $E$ then $\{f>0\}$ is countable; the discontinuities of a monotone function are countable.
  • Binary/decimal expansions: every $x\in[0,1]$ has a binary expansion; dyadic rationals have two. The map $\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N}\to[0,1]$, $(a_n)\mapsto\sum a_n2^{-n}$, is onto but not one-one; with base $10$ and digits $0,1$ it is one-one but not onto.

2. Sequences

  • $a_n\to L$: for every $\epsilon>0$ there is $N$ with $|a_n-L|<\epsilon$ for $n\ge N$. Convergent sequences are bounded.
  • Monotone convergence: a bounded monotone sequence converges (to its sup or inf). For recursions $a_{n+1}=g(a_n)$: show boundedness and monotonicity by induction, then the limit solves $L=g(L)$.
  • Bolzano–Weierstrass: every bounded sequence has a convergent subsequence.
  • Cauchy: $|a_m-a_n|<\epsilon$ for $m,n\ge N$. In $\mathbb R$, Cauchy $\iff$ convergent. Contractions $|a_{n+1}-a_n|\le c|a_n-a_{n-1}|$ ($c<1$) give Cauchy sequences.
  • Limsup/liminf: the largest and smallest subsequential limits; $a_n\to L$ iff $\limsup=\liminf=L$. For continuous $f$: $\liminf f(x_n)\le f(\liminf x_n)$ need not be equality.
  • Standard limits: $n^{1/n}\to1$, $a^{1/n}\to1$ ($a>0$), $\left(1+\frac an\right)^n\to e^a$, $\frac{n^k}{b^n}\to0$ ($b>1$), $(a^n+b^n)^{1/n}\to\max(a,b)$.
  • Cesàro: if $a_n\to L$ then $\frac{a_1+\dots+a_n}n\to L$. Stolz–Cesàro: if $b_n\uparrow\infty$ and $\frac{a_{n+1}-a_n}{b_{n+1}-b_n}\to L$ then $\frac{a_n}{b_n}\to L$.
  • Ratio and root: if $\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\to r$ then $a_n^{1/n}\to r$; $r<1$ gives $a_n\to0$.
  • Linear recurrences: $x_{n+1}=ax_n+bx_{n-1}$ has solutions $\alpha r_1^n+\beta r_2^n$ from $r^2=ar+b$; it converges iff the active roots satisfy $|r|<1$ or $r=1$.
  • Harmonic numbers: $H_n=\log n+\gamma+o(1)$; $\sum_{j=n+1}^{kn}\frac1j\to\log k$.

3. Series

  • $\sum a_n$ converges iff its partial sums converge. Necessary: $a_n\to0$.
  • Geometric: $\sum r^n=\frac1{1-r}$ for $|r|<1$; $\sum nr^n=\frac r{(1-r)^2}$. Telescoping: $\sum(b_n-b_{n+1})=b_1-\lim b_n$.
  • $p$-series: $\sum\frac1{n^p}$ converges iff $p>1$; $\sum\frac1{n(\log n)^q}$ iff $q>1$.
  • Comparison and limit comparison (positive terms): if $\frac{a_n}{b_n}\to c\in(0,\infty)$, they converge together.
  • Ratio test: $\lim\left|\frac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right|<1$ converges, $>1$ diverges. Root test: $\limsup|a_n|^{1/n}<1$ converges.
  • Integral test: for decreasing $f\ge0$, $\sum f(n)$ and $\int_1^\infty f$ converge together; also $\int_n^\infty f\le\sum_{k\ge n}f(k)\le f(n)+\int_n^\infty f$, e.g. $\sum_{k\ge n}\frac1{k^2}\sim\frac1n$.
  • Cauchy condensation: for decreasing $a_n\ge0$, $\sum a_n$ and $\sum2^ka_{2^k}$ converge together.
  • Alternating series (Leibniz): if $b_n\downarrow0$ then $\sum(-1)^nb_n$ converges.
  • Absolute vs conditional: absolute convergence implies convergence; rearrangements of conditionally convergent series can change the sum (Riemann).
  • Dirichlet/Abel: $\sum a_nb_n$ converges if the partial sums of $a_n$ are bounded and $b_n\downarrow0$; or if $\sum a_n$ converges and $b_n$ is monotone and bounded. So $\sum a_n$ convergent $\Rightarrow\sum\frac{a_n}n$ convergent.
  • Products: $\sum a_nb_n$ can diverge when $\sum a_n,\sum b_n$ converge conditionally ($a_n=b_n=\frac{(-1)^n}{\sqrt n}$); it converges absolutely if one converges absolutely and the other's terms are bounded.
  • Cauchy–Schwarz for series: $\sum|a_nb_n|\le\left(\sum a_n^2\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum b_n^2\right)^{1/2}$.

4. Continuity and uniform continuity

  • Sequential criterion: $f$ is continuous at $a$ iff $x_n\to a$ implies $f(x_n)\to f(a)$.
  • Functions defined differently on $\mathbb Q$ and $\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Q$ are continuous exactly where the two formulas agree (by density of both sets).
  • Uniformly continuous: one $\delta$ works for all $x$. Lipschitz ($|f(x)-f(y)|\le K|x-y|$) and Hölder ($\le K|x-y|^\alpha$) functions are uniformly continuous; $\sqrt x$ is uniformly continuous on $[0,\infty)$, $x^2$ and $\sin(x^2)$ on $\mathbb R$ are not.
  • Heine–Cantor: continuous on $[a,b]$ $\Rightarrow$ uniformly continuous.
  • Uniformly continuous on $(a,b)$ $\Rightarrow$ extends continuously to $[a,b]$ (Cauchy sequences go to Cauchy sequences).
  • Uniformly continuous on $\mathbb R$ $\Rightarrow$ $|f(x)|\le A+B|x|$.
  • Monotone functions: one-sided limits exist everywhere; jumps are countable and their sizes add up to at most $f(b)-f(a)$.
  • Functional equations: $f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y)$ with continuity gives $f(x)=cx$; $f(x+1)=f(x)+1$ with continuity gives $f(x)=x+\text{(bounded periodic)}$.
  • Fixed points: a continuous $f:[a,b]\to[a,b]$ has a fixed point. A strict contraction ($|f(x)-f(y)|<|x-y|$) on $[a,b]$ has exactly one.

5. Mean value theorem and Taylor's theorem

  • Rolle, Lagrange MVT, Cauchy MVT: $\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{g(b)-g(a)}=\frac{f'(c)}{g'(c)}$.
  • Consequences: $f'=0$ on an interval $\Rightarrow$ constant (on an open set: constant on each component, so countably many values); $|f'|\le K$ $\Rightarrow$ $K$-Lipschitz; $|f(x)-f(y)|\le|x-y|^{1+\epsilon}$ $\Rightarrow$ $f$ constant.
  • $f$ with $n$ zeros has $f'$ with at least $n-1$ zeros. If $f(x_n)=c$ at points $x_n\to a$, then $f'(a)=0$ (and so on for higher derivatives by Rolle).
  • Darboux: derivatives have the intermediate value property (even when not continuous).
  • If $f\to L$ and $f'\to\alpha$ as $x\to\infty$, then $\alpha=0$. If $f''\to1$ then $\frac{f(x)}{x^2}\to\frac12$.
  • Taylor with Lagrange remainder: $f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^n\frac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}(x-a)^k+\frac{f^{(n+1)}(c)}{(n+1)!}(x-a)^{n+1}$.
  • Symmetric quotients: $\frac{f(h)+f(-h)-2f(0)}{h^2}\to f''(0)$.
  • Smooth but not analytic: $e^{-1/x^2}$ (value $0$ at $0$) has all derivatives $0$ at $0$.
  • Convexity: $f''\ge0$ $\iff$ convex $\iff$ $f(tx+(1-t)y)\le tf(x)+(1-t)f(y)$; Jensen's inequality.

6. Riemann integration and improper integrals

  • Riemann integrable (bounded $f$ on $[a,b]$): upper and lower sums can be made arbitrarily close. Continuous, monotone, and bounded functions with countably many discontinuities are integrable (Lebesgue's criterion: discontinuities of measure zero). $\mathbf 1_{\mathbb Q}$ is not.
  • Fundamental theorem: for integrable $f$, $F(x)=\int_a^xf$ is Lipschitz; $F'(x)=f(x)$ where $f$ is continuous.
  • Mean value theorem for integrals and weighted MVT: $\int fg=f(c)\int g$ for continuous $f$ and $g\ge0$.
  • Moments determine oddness/evenness: if $\int_{-1}^1f(x)x^{2n}dx=0$ for all $n$ (continuous $f$), Weierstrass approximation gives $f$ odd.
  • Improper integrals: $\int_1^\infty x^{-p}$ converges iff $p>1$; $\int_0^1x^{-p}$ iff $p<1$. Compare $\frac{|f|}{|g|}\sim cx^{m-n}$ for polynomials: $\int_1^\infty$ converges iff $n-m\ge2$.
  • Absolute vs conditional: $\int_1^\infty\frac{\sin x}x$ converges, $\int_1^\infty\frac{|\sin x|}x$ diverges.
  • Averages: if $\int_n^{n+1}f\to c$ then $\frac1T\int_0^Tf\to c$ (Cesàro).
  • Concentration: $(n+1)\int_0^1x^nf(x)\,dx\to f(1)$; $\int_0^1\frac{n}{1+n^2x^2}g(x)\,dx\to\frac\pi2g(0)$.

7. Sequences and series of functions

  • Pointwise: $f_n(x)\to f(x)$ for each $x$. Uniform: $\sup_x|f_n(x)-f(x)|\to0$.
  • To test uniformity: compute $M_n=\sup|f_n-f|$ (calculus: maximise $f_n-f$). If the limit is discontinuous but the $f_n$ are continuous, convergence is not uniform. Moving bumps ($f_n$ peaking at $\frac1n$ with height not tending to $0$) converge pointwise but not uniformly.
  • Uniform limits: of continuous functions are continuous; $\int f_n\to\int f$ on $[a,b]$. Derivatives need more: if $f_n'\to g$ uniformly and $f_n(x_0)$ converges, then $f_n\to f$ uniformly with $f'=g$.
  • Uniform convergence on $(a,b)$ of continuous functions on $[a,b]$ extends to $[a,b]$.
  • Dini: monotone convergence of continuous functions to a continuous limit on a compact set is uniform. (Decreasing limits of continuous increasing functions are right-continuous.)
  • Weierstrass M-test: $|u_n(x)|\le M_n$ with $\sum M_n<\infty$ $\Rightarrow$ $\sum u_n$ converges uniformly.
  • Power series $\sum a_n(x-c)^n$: radius $R=\frac1{\limsup|a_n|^{1/n}}$ (or $\lim\left|\frac{a_n}{a_{n+1}}\right|$). Uniform on $|x-c|\le r<R$; differentiate and integrate term by term inside. Check the endpoints separately: $\sum\frac{x^n}n$ converges on $[-1,1)$, not uniformly; $\sum\frac{x^n}{n^2}$ converges on $[-1,1]$ but its derivative blows up at $1$.
  • Weierstrass approximation: polynomials are dense in $C[a,b]$ (sup norm).
