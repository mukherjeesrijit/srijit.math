1. Methods of estimation

A statistic is a function of the sample; an estimator θ^ is a statistic used to guess θ.

Method of moments. Equate sample moments to population moments and solve: Xˉ=Eθ​[X], n1​∑Xi2​=Eθ​[X2], …

Maximum likelihood. Maximise L(θ)=∏f(xi​;θ), usually via ℓ=logL and ℓ′(θ)=0.

  • Check the support. When the support depends on θ (Uniform(0,θ), e−(x−θ) for x>θ), L is maximised at an endpoint, e.g. θ^=X(n)​ or X(1)​, not at a zero of the derivative.
  • Discrete parameter: compare L(θ+1)/L(θ) with 1, or take the largest allowed value.
  • Invariance: the MLE of g(θ) is g(θ^). (Median of Exp with mean μ: μ^​log2=Xˉlog2.)

Standard MLEs: Bernoulli p^​=Xˉ; Poisson λ^=Xˉ; Exp (rate) λ^=Xˉ1​; Normal μ^​=Xˉ, σ^2=n1​∑(Xi​−Xˉ)2; Uniform(0,θ) θ^=X(n)​.

  • Unbiased: Eθ​[θ^]=θ. S2=n−11​∑(Xi​−Xˉ)2 is unbiased for σ2; n1​∑(Xi​−Xˉ)2 is not. For N(0,σ2), n1​∑Xi2​ is unbiased.

3. Sufficiency, completeness, UMVUE

  • Sufficient: T is sufficient if the conditional law of the sample given T does not depend on θ.
  • Factorisation theorem: T is sufficient iff f(x;θ)=g(T(x);θ)h(x). Indicators of the support count: for Uniform(θ−1,θ)-type families the sufficient statistic is (min,max) of the suitably shifted observations.
  • A one-to-one function of a sufficient statistic is sufficient.
  • Exponential family f=h(x)c(θ)eη(θ)T(x): ∑T(Xi​) is sufficient and (if η ranges over an interval) complete. For N(0,σ2): ∑Xi2​.
  • Complete: Eθ​[g(T)]=0 for all θ forces g(T)=0. (∑Xi​ for N(0,σ2) is not complete: its mean is 0 for every σ.)
  • Minimal sufficient: T(x)=T(y) iff f(y;θ)f(x;θ)​ does not depend on θ.
  • Ancillary: a statistic whose distribution does not depend on θ. Basu: a complete sufficient statistic is independent of every ancillary statistic.
  • Rao–Blackwell: E[W∣T] for sufficient T improves an unbiased W.
  • Lehmann–Scheffé: an unbiased function of a complete sufficient statistic is the unique UMVUE.

4. Confidence intervals

A 100(1−α)% confidence interval (L,U) satisfies Pθ​(L≤θ≤U)=1−α for every θ.

  • Pivotal method: find Q(X,θ) whose distribution is free of θ, take its quantiles, and solve for θ.
  • Normal mean, σ known: Xˉ±zα/2​n​σ​. Sample size for half-width d: n=(dzα/2​σ​)2 (with z0.025​=1.96).
  • Normal mean, σ unknown: Xˉ±tn−1,α/2​n​S​. Variance: (χn−1,α/22​(n−1)S2​,χn−1,1−α/22​(n−1)S2​).
  • Exponential mean μ: μ2∑Xi​​∼χ2n2​.
  • Location families like e−(x−θ), x>θ: X(1)​−θ∼ Exp(n), so P(X(1)​−θ≤b)=1−e−nb gives intervals [X(1)​−b,X(1)​].

5. Hypothesis testing

  • Errors: type I = rejecting a true H0​ (probability α, the size); type II = accepting a false H0​. Power =P(reject H0​∣H1​).
  • Test function: ϕ(x)∈[0,1] is the probability of rejecting; randomise on a boundary to hit the size exactly (needed for discrete data).
  • Neyman–Pearson lemma: for simple H0​:f0​ vs H1​:f1​, the most powerful size-α test rejects when f0​(x)f1​(x)​>k (randomising when =k). In a table, order the points by f1​/f0​ and add them until the size is reached.
  • Monotone likelihood ratio: if f(x;θ0​)f(x;θ1​)​ increases in T(x) for θ1​>θ0​, then "reject when T>c" is UMP for H0​:θ≤θ0​ vs H1​:θ>θ0​. (Exponential families with increasing η.)
  • Likelihood ratio test: reject when Λ=supLsupH0​​L​ is small; −2logΛ≈χd2​ for large n.
  • Standard tests: z-test (known σ), t-test (unknown σ), χ2-test for variance, F-test for two variances.
  • p-value: the probability, under H0​, of data at least as extreme as observed; reject when p≤α.
